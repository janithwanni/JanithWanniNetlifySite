Research

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Econometrics & Business Statistics at Monash University, supervised by Dianne Cook, Patricia Menendez, Thiyanga Talagala and Kate Saunders. My research asks how we can see what explainable AI methods are telling us, and whether simpler, interpretable models can stand in for complex ones.

PhD thesis

Explaining AI through visual explanations and efficient interpretable models

Local explainers such as LIME, SHAP, Anchors and counterfactuals each describe a different facet of a model, and they often disagree. The thesis places their explanations back in the data space, where they can be explored with high-dimensional visualisation such as tours. It rebuilds two explainers in R so that every intermediate step can be inspected, and it shows that small, well-chosen models can rival larger ones.

Visual explanations of the explainers

Draws SHAP, LIME, counterfactuals and Anchors geometrically in the data space so they can be compared side by side. In the same model they often name different features as most important.

Random seeds, stability & parsimony

Single-layer neural networks show that simple models can match complex ones but are harder to find: their accuracy swings with the random seed. The project comes with a Shiny teaching tool.

kultarr: Anchors, revisited

A pure-R implementation of Anchors that you can debug and visualise. It uses S7 classes, memoised rewards and parallel search, and includes helpers for drawing anchors in high dimensions.

R package · in development

Chapter · Docs · Blog post

kumquat: a simpler LIME

A transparent take on LIME for tabular data. It returns the perturbations, predictions and local GLM, so you can audit exactly how an explanation was built.

My undergraduate thesis. It generates scatterplots that match target scagnostic measures by placing points with simulated annealing, which gives data for testing visual and ML methods.
